# Social impact — who is underserved, and what improves

Underserved population: older adults and people with low health
literacy in underserved communities, including people who cannot
reliably read or spell a prescription's generic name, and people
without a pharmacist readily available to explain a new medicine.

The problem, stated plainly: FDA labels are written for clinicians.
Interaction warnings are split across sections and are asymmetric
between the two drugs' labels. A text-only checker that demands a
correctly spelled generic name excludes the very people most at risk
from a misunderstood prescription.

What ClearDose changes, measurably (to be measured in a real pilot,
not claimed here): the number of steps between
a person holding a new bottle and understanding (a) what it is for,
(b) whether its label mentions a medicine they already take, and
(c) what to ask a pharmacist. The agent reduces that to one
conversation — a name, or a photo — and returns the label's own words
under plain-language headings, and a question list to take to the
pharmacist. No reading-level reduction is claimed: the label text
itself is not rewritten or simplified.

No impact numbers are claimed in this repository. No pilot, user
study, or outcome measurement has been run. The measurable-improvement
claim in the challenge rubric is addressed by the design (fewer
steps, both-label checking) and by the demo,
which shows the full journey in under three minutes. Any future pilot
should measure: task completion without help, time to first correct
understanding of a flag, and whether users take the question list to
a pharmacist — with consent, privacy, and clinician review built in
before any real deployment.
